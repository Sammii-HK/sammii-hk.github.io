Astral Guide grounded in real data

The Astral Guide works because it is grounded in chart calculations, personal transits, moon phase context, tarot history, journalling patterns, and grimoire retrieval, not generic AI output.

4 min readaiastrologyretrievalproduct

The failure mode of AI in astrology apps is almost always the same: generic output dressed up as personalisation.

Put "Sun in Aries, Moon in Virgo" into a prompt and you will get something that sounds plausible and applies to millions of people. The system technically used personal data. The output does not actually feel personal.

That is the grounding problem.

It is also why most AI features in this category feel hollow after the first few interactions.

What grounding means here

For the Astral Guide, grounding means the model should not be the source of astrological truth.

The source of truth should be:

  • the user's real birth chart
  • the current transits affecting that chart
  • the current moon phase and cosmic context
  • tarot history
  • journal and mood patterns
  • the grimoire knowledge base

Then the model can do what it is good at, connect, synthesise, explain.

That ordering matters.

The three-layer structure

The structure that works for Lunary has three layers.

1. Compute the chart accurately

The first layer is deterministic. Planetary positions, houses, aspects, patterns, all of that comes from calculation. The chart is not inferred. It is computed.

That means the system knows what is actually present before any language model gets involved.

2. Add live context

The second layer is timing.

Current transits, moon phase, and other active conditions tell the system what is happening now, not just what the chart says in principle. This is what keeps the Astral Guide from becoming a static personality explainer.

It can answer questions in the present tense.

3. Retrieve the right knowledge

The third layer is retrieval.

Lunary's grimoire contains the interpretive and ritual material. The model is given the relevant references for the question and the chart state. That might include astrological meanings, correspondences, tarot context, crystal or ritual recommendations, or numerology links when relevant.

This is the point most products skip.

Without retrieval, the model fills the gaps with generic astrology. With retrieval, it has something grounded to think with.

Why this improves reliability

The model is no longer being asked to generate astrology from memory. It is being asked to synthesise known context.

That lowers cost and improves reliability at the same time.

It also lets the same system answer different kinds of questions without pretending they are all the same task. A moon phase question does not need deep natal analysis. A transit question does. A tarot pattern question should include journal history and recent cards. The context can be loaded conditionally.

That is how the Astral Guide avoids becoming expensive theatre.

The role of the grimoire

The grimoire is the part that turns the guide from an interface into a system.

It gives the model a body of trusted material to work from. Not random internet astrology, not synthetic filler, actual internal knowledge that can be matched to the user's chart state.

That is what makes it possible to move from vague advice toward specific synthesis.

The answer does not need to be "Mars means drive." It can be "this transit is activating this part of your chart, here is how that intersects with what has been showing up in your journal, and here is the symbolic framework that fits it."

What I think most people get wrong

They think the trick is prompt engineering.

The real trick is context engineering.

Once the underlying chart data, timing data, and retrieval layer are solid, the prompt can stay relatively straightforward. Without those layers, no prompt is good enough to rescue the output.

That is the part that made the Astral Guide feel usable to me. Not that it could always say something. That it had enough real structure underneath the language to say something worth asking for.

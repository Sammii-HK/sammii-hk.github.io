How I built Lunary's birth chart reader

Lunary's birth chart reader works because the model is not inventing astrology from scratch. It is grounded in a real natal chart, live transits, and grimoire retrieval, then asked to synthesise from there.

4 min readaiastrologyllmproduct

The hardest problem in building an AI interpretation layer for a birth chart is not the AI. It is grounding.

Left alone, a language model will produce generic astrology. "Sun in Scorpio people are intense." That is not wrong. It is also not useful to a specific person looking at their own chart. The real challenge is forcing the model to reason about one chart, one moment, one actual configuration, rather than the archetype in the abstract.

That is the difference between an astrology chatbot and a birth chart reader.

The wrong way to build it

Most AI astrology products make the same mistake. They pass a handful of placements into the prompt, maybe Sun, Moon, Rising, then ask the model to generate insight. The model responds with the statistical average of what those placements usually mean.

That output feels plausible for a sentence or two. It falls apart the moment you want precision.

It cannot tell the difference between Mercury in Virgo in the 3rd house trine Saturn and Mercury in Virgo in the 12th house square Mars. Those are not small differences. They change the whole shape of the reading.

If the input is shallow, the output will be shallow too.

What Lunary gives the model instead

Lunary starts with a real chart, not a summary.

That means the model gets:

  • every planet
  • every sign
  • every degree
  • every house placement
  • every aspect and its orb
  • current transits affecting that chart
  • moon phase and other current cosmic context

The app itself handles the hard astronomical work first. Lunary is not an "AI astrology app" in the usual sense. The chart, the transits, the pattern detection, the timing, all of that is calculation.

The AI layer comes afterwards.

Its job is to read the structure that already exists.

Retrieval matters more than generation

The second part is the grimoire.

Lunary has its own body of reference material, astrology, tarot, rituals, correspondences, crystal work, numerology, all structured and queryable. The birth chart reader does not just hand a natal chart to the model and hope for the best. It retrieves relevant grimoire context matched to the placements and themes in that specific chart.

That changes the role of the model completely.

Instead of inventing astrological meaning from latent memory, it is synthesising between:

  • the user's actual chart
  • the user's current transits
  • the current lunar and cosmic context
  • the relevant grimoire material already written for those patterns

That is a much narrower, much more reliable task.

Why the output feels different

When the input is genuinely personal, the response reads differently.

Instead of "Mercury in Virgo means you communicate carefully", the model can reason about Mercury in Virgo in a specific house, with a specific aspect pattern, while a current transit is activating that part of the chart. It can connect present timing to natal structure.

That is where the reading starts feeling like it belongs to one person rather than everyone with the same Sun sign.

The difference is not tone. It is architecture.

The context stack

The current Astral Guide context in Lunary pulls from multiple layers:

  • birth chart data
  • personal transit data
  • moon phase and current cosmic context
  • tarot history
  • journal and mood patterns
  • retrieved grimoire recommendations

That stack is what lets the reader move beyond static chart interpretation.

If someone asks why a particular card keeps appearing, or why a period feels unusually intense, the system can look at the chart, the transits, recent patterns, and the relevant symbolic material together. The answer has somewhere real to come from.

The actual design principle

The model's job is synthesis, not recall.

That one decision solves most of the quality problem.

If you ask a model to recall astrology, you get generic astrology. If you ask it to synthesise a chart, transits, history, and retrieved reference material, you get something much closer to a proper reading.

That is the architecture I trust.

What I would not call this

I would not call it AI-generated astrology.

That framing suggests the model is authoring the meaning from scratch. It is not. The calculation layer, the chart structure, the transit timing, and the retrieved knowledge do the heavy lifting. The model connects them into language a person can actually use.

That is a much less magical claim. It is also a much truer one.

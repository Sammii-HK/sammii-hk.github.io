A real numerical process model, with deliberately simplified worlds. Height, deposits and recent activity are calculated over time. Terrain is generated; rates are accelerated and uncalibrated. The time axis does not reconstruct the actual past or forecast the future of these bodies.

What changes, and what this model leaves out

Surface height evolves through conservative spherical diffusion, bowl-and-rim impacts, local deposition that fills some older relief, and simple uplift on selected rocky worlds. A one-variable heat balance controls activity. Every stored snapshot is an actual model state; scrubbing does not recalculate a decorative animation.

The model uses an equal-area longitude grid, 128 × 64 cells, 180 time steps and stable diffusion substeps. Cooling is an exponential approach to a prescribed heating equilibrium. Impacts follow a declining synthetic event rate. Deposition and impact sizes are illustrative. The highest-resolution display interpolates this coarse grid and exaggerates slopes for readability.

It does not solve plate tectonics, interior fluid dynamics, impact shock physics, atmosphere, climate, orbital tides or biological evolution. The icy fracture lines are an artistic surface cue. Internal heat is a dimensionless index, not a measured core temperature. “Recently changed” counts cells above a decaying activity threshold.
